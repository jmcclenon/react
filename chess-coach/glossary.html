Chess Glossary

A plain-English reference to the terms you will meet at the board and in the coach's feedback — the rules, tactics, pawn structures, positional ideas, and endgame concepts that make up the language of chess. Use the search box or jump to a letter.

No terms match your search.

A

Algebraic Notation
The standard way to record moves, naming each square by its file (a–h) and rank (1–8) — e.g. Nf3 means a knight to f3.
Alekhine's Gun
A heavy battery of two rooks stacked on a file with the queen behind them — maximum pressure down one line.
Annotation
Written commentary and symbols (!, ?, !?, ⩲, …) that evaluate moves and positions.
Attack
Coordinated pressure against a target — a piece, a pawn, a weak square, or the enemy king.

B

Back-rank Mate
Checkmate along the opponent's first rank when their king is trapped behind its own unmoved pawns.
Backward Pawn
A pawn left behind its neighbours that no friendly pawn can defend and that cannot safely advance — a lasting weakness.
Battery
Two or more pieces lined up on the same file, rank, or diagonal (e.g. queen + bishop) to multiply their force.
Bishop Pair
Holding both bishops while the opponent has at most one. In open positions the pair sweeps long diagonals and is worth roughly half a pawn.
Blitz
A fast time control, typically 3–5 minutes per side.
Blockade
Parking a piece — ideally a knight — directly in front of an enemy passed pawn so it cannot advance.
Blunder ??
A serious mistake that loses material or the game outright.
Book Move
An established opening move drawn from known theory.
Bullet
Very fast chess, usually 1 minute (sometimes 2) per side.

C

Candidate Move
A move you single out to calculate during your thinking process.
Castling O-O / O-O-O
A once-per-game king-and-rook move for king safety: kingside (O-O) or queenside (O-O-O). Neither piece may have moved, and the king can't pass through check.
Centralization
Placing pieces near the centre, where they control the most squares and switch flanks fastest.
Check
A direct attack on the king that must be answered immediately (block, capture, or move the king).
Checkmate
The king is in check with no legal way out — the game ends.
Combination
A forcing sequence, often involving a sacrifice, that reaches a concrete goal by calculation.
Connected Pawns
Pawns on adjacent files that can defend one another.
Connected Passed Pawns
Adjacent passed pawns — a powerful, often winning, duo in the endgame.
Counterplay
Active play that creates your own threats instead of merely defending against the opponent's.
Cramped
A position with little space, where the pieces get in each other's way.

D

Decoy
Luring an enemy piece onto a square where it can be exploited.
Deflection
Forcing a defending piece away from the duty it is performing.
Desperado
A piece that is already lost grabbing material (or forcing a trade) before it disappears.
Development
Bringing your pieces off the back rank into active, useful positions — a core opening goal.
Discovered Attack
Moving one piece to unveil an attack from a piece standing behind it.
Discovered Check
A discovered attack that delivers check — often devastating because the moving piece is free to strike elsewhere.
Double Check
Check from two pieces at once. The only legal reply is to move the king.
Doubled Pawns
Two friendly pawns on the same file — usually a weakness, though they can open files in return.
Draw
A game with no winner: by stalemate, threefold repetition, the fifty-move rule, insufficient material, or agreement.

E

ECO
The Encyclopaedia of Chess Openings, which classifies every opening with a code from A00 to E99.
Elo
The rating system that measures relative playing strength; higher is stronger.
En Passant "in passing"
A special capture: if an enemy pawn advances two squares and lands beside your pawn, you may capture it as if it had moved only one — but only on the very next move.
En Prise
A piece left undefended and free to be taken.
Endgame
The final phase, with few pieces left, where king activity and pawns decide the outcome.
Exchange (the)
Winning a rook for a minor piece is "winning the exchange." Lower-case "an exchange" also just means a trade of pieces.

F

Fianchetto
Developing a bishop to b2, g2, b7, or g7 after moving the knight-pawn — aiming it along the long diagonal.
FEN
Forsyth–Edwards Notation: a single line of text that fully describes a position (used to set up puzzles and share positions).
File
A vertical column of squares, labelled a to h.
Fifty-move Rule
Either player may claim a draw if 50 consecutive moves pass with no capture and no pawn move.
Flag
Running out of time. To "flag" someone is to win because their clock hit zero.
Fork
A single piece attacking two or more targets at once — the knight is the classic forker.
Fortress
A defensive setup the stronger side simply cannot break down, holding a draw despite being down material.

G

Gambit
An opening that deliberately sacrifices material — usually a pawn — for faster development, the initiative, or an attack.
Good Bishop
A bishop whose movement is not blocked by its own pawns (the opposite of a bad bishop).
Grandmaster GM
The highest lifetime title awarded by FIDE.

H

Half-open File
A file with pawns of only one colour — excellent for a rook to press against the lone pawn.
Hanging Pawns
Two adjacent friendly pawns (typically on c- and d-files) with no pawns on the files beside them — dynamic but vulnerable.
Hanging Piece
An undefended piece that can be captured for free.
Hole
A square in your camp that no pawn can ever defend again — a natural home (outpost) for an enemy piece.

I

In-between Move Zwischenzug
An unexpected move — usually a check or threat — slipped in before making the "obvious" recapture or reply.
Initiative
The power to make threats and dictate the flow of the game.
Interference
Interposing a piece to cut the line between an enemy piece and what it guards.
Isolated Pawn Isolani
A pawn with no friendly pawns on either adjacent file — it must be defended by pieces and the square in front is a weakness.
IQP
The Isolated Queen's Pawn — a common structure that gives active piece play in return for a long-term weakness.

J

J'adoube "I adjust"
Said aloud before touching a piece to re-centre it, so the touch-move rule does not force you to play it.

K

Key Square
A square whose control decides the position — especially in king-and-pawn endgames.
Kingside
The half of the board nearest the kings' starting side, files e–h.
Kingside Castling O-O
Castling toward the h-file — the fastest, most common route to safety.

L

Luft German: "air"
A breathing hole made by nudging a pawn in front of the castled king, so a rook check on the back rank isn't mate.
Lucena Position
A fundamental winning technique in rook endgames — "building a bridge" to shepherd a pawn home.

M

Major Piece
A queen or a rook.
Material
The total value of your pieces. To be "up material" is to have more than the opponent.
Middlegame
The phase after the opening — the richest ground for tactics, attacks, and planning.
Minority Attack
Advancing your fewer pawns against the opponent's majority to create a weakness — the classic plan against the Queen's Gambit Declined.
Minor Piece
A bishop or a knight.

N

Notation
Any system for recording a game; algebraic notation is today's standard.

O

Open File
A file with no pawns on it — prime real estate for a rook.
Open Game
Openings beginning 1.e4 e5, which tend to lead to early piece contact.
Opening
The first phase of the game, focused on development, the centre, and king safety.
Opposite-coloured Bishops
Each side has one bishop, on opposite colours. Notoriously drawish in endgames, but a powerful attacking edge in the middlegame.
Opposition
A king-versus-king standoff a knight's move apart; the side not to move usually holds the advantage.
Outpost
An advanced square, ideally protected by a pawn and safe from enemy pawns — a dream home for a knight.
Overloading
Giving one piece too many defensive jobs, so removing or distracting it collapses the position.

P

Passed Pawn
A pawn with no enemy pawns ahead of it on its own or adjacent files — nothing can stop it except pieces.
Pawn Chain
A diagonal row of pawns each defending the next; attack it at its base.
Pawn Storm
Rolling your pawns forward to pry open the shelter around the enemy king.
Pawn Structure
The overall skeleton of pawns, which dictates the plans and piece placement for both sides.
Perpetual Check
An unstoppable series of checks that repeats the position and forces a draw.
PGN
Portable Game Notation — the standard text format for storing complete games (as exported by the Copy PGN button).
Pin
An attack in which a piece cannot move without exposing a more valuable one behind it. Against the king it is an absolute pin (illegal to move).
Ply
A single move by one side — half of a full move.
Prophylaxis
A move that quietly prevents the opponent's plan before you pursue your own.
Promotion
A pawn that reaches the last rank turns into a queen, rook, bishop, or knight.
Protected Passed Pawn
A passed pawn defended by another pawn — very hard to blockade or win.

Q

Queenside
The half of the board on the a–d files.
Queenside Castling O-O-O
Castling toward the a-file; slower than kingside but places the rook on a central file.
Quiet Move
A non-forcing move — no check, capture, or immediate threat — that is often the surprising point of a combination.

R

Rank
A horizontal row of squares, numbered 1 to 8.
Rapid
A medium time control, roughly 15–60 minutes per side.
Removing the Defender
Capturing or driving off the very piece that guards your target.
Repetition Threefold
If the same position (same pieces, same side to move, same rights) occurs three times, either player may claim a draw.
Rook Lift
Swinging a rook up and along a rank (e.g. Re1–e3–g3) to bring it into the attack.

S

Sacrifice
Giving up material for a greater return — an attack, the initiative, or checkmate.
SAN
Standard Algebraic Notation, the usual written form of moves (Nf3, exd5, O-O, Qxh7#).
Simplification
Trading pieces to reach a clearer, favourable position — a standard way to convert an advantage.
Skewer
Like a pin reversed: the more valuable piece stands in front and, when it moves, the piece behind it is captured.
Smothered Mate
A checkmate by a knight when the king is completely boxed in by its own pieces.
Space
The amount of the board your pawns and pieces control; more space means more freedom to manoeuvre.
Stalemate
The player to move has no legal move but is not in check — the game is drawn.
Swindle
Rescuing a lost position with a well-timed trick or trap.

T

Tabiya
A well-known "standard" position that many games in an opening funnel into before real thinking begins.
Tempo
A unit of time equal to one move. You "gain a tempo" when you develop while making a threat the opponent must answer.
Threat
A move that, if allowed next, would win material or deliver mate.
Time Control
The clock rules for a game — a base time plus an optional increment added each move.
Touch-move
In serious play, if you touch one of your pieces you must move it (and a touched enemy piece must be captured if legal).
Transposition
Reaching the same position through a different order of moves.
Triangulation
A king manoeuvre that loses a single tempo on purpose to hand the opponent a losing zugzwang.

U

Underpromotion
Promoting to something other than a queen — usually a knight for a fork or check, or a rook/bishop to avoid stalemate.
Undermining
Attacking the pawn or piece that supports an enemy structure so the whole thing falls.

V

Variation
A particular branch of moves within an opening or a piece of analysis.

W

Weak Square
A square that can no longer be guarded by a pawn — an invitation for an enemy piece.
Windmill
A repeating see-saw of discovered checks that mows down enemy material.

X

X-ray
A piece's influence that passes "through" another piece on the same line — used to attack or to defend from behind.

Z

Zugzwang German: "compulsion to move"
A position where you would love to pass — but every legal move makes things worse.
Zwischenzug
See In-between Move — an unexpected reply inserted before the expected one.
