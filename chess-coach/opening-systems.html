Play Glossary Opening Systems

Opening Systems

A guided tour of the openings the coach and the AI personalities play — grouped into three tiers by how much theory they demand. Start with the Basic systems to build good habits, graduate to the Moderate classics, then explore the sharp, theory-heavy Advanced battlegrounds. Each entry gives the idea behind the opening, the middlegame strategy for both sides, and practical hints. Moves are shown in standard notation.

Basic systems Beginner-friendly

Principle-based openings that almost play themselves: fast development, a solid centre, early castling. Easy to learn, hard to go wrong — the best place to start.

Italian Game

C50White
1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4
Explanation

The most natural opening in chess: grab the centre, develop the knight with a threat, and aim the bishop straight at f7 — Black's most vulnerable square. The "Giuoco Piano" ("quiet game") that follows is calm and instructive.

Strategy
  • White: build the big centre with c3 and d4, castle, and play on the kingside.
  • Black: mirror with …Bc5 or …Be7, …Nf6, …d6, and challenge the centre with …d5 when ready.
  • Fight for the d4/d5 break — whoever plays it well gets the freer game.
Hints
  • Don't rush b4 or g4 lunges — finish developing first.
  • Watch the f7/f2 squares; a knight on g5 hitting f7 is the oldest trap in the book.
  • Aim for c3 + d4; it is the whole point of the bishop on c4.

Scotch Game

C45White
1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.d4
Explanation

White strikes in the centre immediately, trading the d-pawn for the e-pawn to open the position and get pieces out fast. A great practical weapon that sidesteps heavy Ruy López theory.

Strategy
  • White: recapture on d4 with the knight, develop naturally, and enjoy easy piece play and a lead in development.
  • Black: hit the d4-knight with …Nf6 and …Bb4/…Bc5, then free the game with …d5.
Hints
  • After 3…exd4 4.Nxd4, don't be afraid to trade on c6 to damage Black's pawns.
  • Keep the queen safe — early …Qh4 or Qf6 ideas can cost you tempi if mishandled.

Queen's Gambit

D06White
1.d4 d5 2.c4
Explanation

Not a real gambit — if Black grabs the c4-pawn, White easily wins it back. White offers the c-pawn to deflect Black's d5-pawn and build a dominant centre. The backbone of classical chess.

Strategy
  • White: develop with Nc3, Nf3, Bg5/Bf4, e3, and use the extra central space; the minority attack (b4–b5) is a classic plan.
  • Black (Declined, …e6): solid but slightly passive; free the light-squared bishop and aim for …c5 or …e5 breaks.
  • Black (Accepted, …dxc4): give back the centre for quick development and …c5.
Hints
  • Don't try to cling to the c4-pawn as Black — return it and develop.
  • As White, avoid playing e4 too early before you're fully developed.

London System

D02White
1.d4 d5 2.Bf4 (or 2.Nf3 & 3.Bf4)
Explanation

A "system" you can play on autopilot against almost anything: the same easy setup every game — Bf4, e3, Nf3, Bd3, c3, Nbd2, and short castling. Low theory, rock-solid, and surprisingly venomous.

Strategy
  • White: complete the setup, then choose a plan — kingside attack with Ne5 + f4 + Qf3/Rf3, or a quiet queenside squeeze.
  • Black: challenge the dark-squared bishop with …Bd6 or …Qb6 hitting b2, and strike with …c5.
Hints
  • Play c3 before your knight blocks it, so b2 and the bishop stay defended.
  • The knight jump Ne5 is the heart of White's attacking plans.
  • Meet …Qb6 calmly with Qc1 or Qb3 — don't panic about b2.

Scandinavian Defense

B01Black
1.e4 d5 2.exd5 Qxd5 3.Nc3 Qa5
Explanation

Black challenges the e4-pawn on move one and reaches a clear, easy-to-understand position. You get the same structure nearly every game, which makes it a great first defence to 1.e4.

Strategy
  • Black: develop with …Nf6, …c6 (a safe home for the queen), …Bf5 or …Bg4, …e6 and castle. Solid and structurally sound.
  • White: gain time chasing the queen, grab space with d4, and develop actively.
Hints
  • Tuck the queen back to a5 or d6, and play …c6 to give it an escape square.
  • Develop the light-squared bishop before playing …e6 so it doesn't get trapped.

Caro-Kann Defense

B10Black
1.e4 c6 2.d4 d5
Explanation

A rock-solid answer to 1.e4. Like the French, Black challenges the centre with …d5 — but supports it with the c-pawn instead of the e-pawn, so the light-squared bishop stays free.

Strategy
  • Black: get the "good" bishop outside the pawn chain with …Bf5 or …Bg4, then …e6, …Nd7, and castle. Aim for a healthy structure and a safe endgame.
  • White: choose Advance (e5), Exchange, or the Classical main lines to fight for space.
Hints
  • Always develop the c8-bishop before locking it in with …e6.
  • The Caro rewards patience — you often win in the endgame thanks to the sounder pawns.

Moderate systems Club level

The great classical openings. They reward understanding over memorisation but do have real theory and typical middlegame plans worth learning.

Ruy López (Spanish)

C60White
1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5
Explanation

The most respected 1.e4 e5 opening. The bishop pressures the knight that defends e5, posing long-term questions. Rich, strategic, and played at every level from club to world championship.

Strategy
  • White: after a6/Ba4, tuck the bishop to b3–c2 ("the Spanish bishop"), play c3 & d4, and manoeuvre knights via Nbd2–f1–g3. A slow build toward a kingside attack.
  • Black: the Closed lines (…Be7, …O-O, …d6, …Na5) or the sturdy Berlin (…Nf6) trade solidity for time.
Hints
  • 3…a6 (the Morphy) is the main move; it "puts the question" to the bishop.
  • Don't grab e5 too early — often it's a trap that loses the pawn back with interest.
  • Patience: the Ruy is a manoeuvring battle, not a shootout.

Sicilian Defense

B20–B99Black
1.e4 c5
Explanation

The most popular and most combative answer to 1.e4. Instead of symmetry, Black fights for the centre with a flank pawn, unbalancing the game and playing for a win. It is a whole world of openings — the Najdorf, Dragon, Sveshnikov, Taimanov, Classical and more branch out from here.

Strategy
  • Black: trade the c-pawn for White's d-pawn, giving a half-open c-file for counterplay, then attack on the queenside while White attacks the king.
  • White (Open, 2.Nf3 & 3.d4): use the central and development lead to attack the king — often with f3, Be3, Qd2, O-O-O and a kingside pawn storm.
  • White (Anti-Sicilians): the Alapin (2.c3), Closed (2.Nc3), or Grand Prix (f4) avoid heavy theory.
Hints
  • It is a race: both sides castle on opposite wings and push pawns at each other's king. Count the tempi.
  • The half-open c-file is Black's highway — pile rooks and the queen on it.
  • If you don't want to memorise sharp theory, an Anti-Sicilian is a fine practical choice.

French Defense

C00Black
1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5
Explanation

A solid, strategic defence: Black challenges e4 straight away and accepts a slightly cramped but very sound position, with a clear plan of counterattacking White's centre.

Strategy
  • Black: undermine the centre with …c5 (and sometimes …f6), develop the kingside, and activate the famously "bad" c8-bishop.
  • White: gain space (Advance e5), or open lines (Exchange/Tarrasch/Classical) and target the kingside.
Hints
  • …c5 is almost always the key freeing break — prepare it and play it.
  • Solve the light-squared bishop early (…b6/…Ba6, or …Bd7–b5) so it doesn't stay a spectator.

Slav Defense

D10Black
1.d4 d5 2.c4 c6
Explanation

One of the soundest ways to meet the Queen's Gambit. Black supports d5 with the c-pawn (not the e-pawn), so the light-squared bishop keeps its freedom — a key improvement over the QGD.

Strategy
  • Black: develop the bishop to f5 or g4 before …e6, then complete development and look for …dxc4 followed by …b5 to hold the pawn or …c5/…e5 breaks.
  • White: claim space, develop harmoniously, and pressure the queenside.
Hints
  • The golden rule: get the c8-bishop out before you play …e6.
  • …dxc4 followed by …b5 grabs a pawn — but only when you can hold it; otherwise develop.

English Opening

A10White
1.c4
Explanation

A flexible flank opening: White fights for d5 from the side and often fianchettoes the king's bishop. It frequently transposes into Queen's Pawn structures, so it pairs well with 1.d4.

Strategy
  • White: play g3, Bg2, Nc3, Nf3 and control the centre with pieces; expand with b4 on the queenside or d4 in the centre when it suits.
  • Black: stake a central claim with …e5 (a "reversed Sicilian") or …d5, or play symmetrically with …c5.
Hints
  • The g2-bishop is your best piece — build the position around its long diagonal.
  • Stay flexible; the English rewards good move-order sense over rote memory.

King's Indian Attack

A07White
1.Nf3, g3, Bg2, O-O, d3, Nbd2, e4
Explanation

A "system" like the London but for White with the king's bishop fianchettoed: the same reliable setup versus almost any Black reply. Low theory, and it channels into a thematic kingside attack.

Strategy
  • White: complete the setup, play e4–e5 to gain space, then storm the kingside with Nf1–h2/g4, and pawns to h4/f4.
  • Black: expand on the queenside where White is not attacking, and race for counterplay.
Hints
  • The e5-push is the signal to switch on the kingside attack.
  • Reroute the d2-knight via f1 to g3 or e3 to join the assault.

Advanced systems Theory-heavy

Sharp, deeply analysed openings where concrete knowledge matters. Hugely rewarding — and what the Grandmaster personalities will throw at you — but best tackled once the fundamentals are second nature.

Sicilian Najdorf

B90Black
1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 d6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nf6 5.Nc3 a6
Explanation

The most famous and most analysed opening in chess — Fischer and Kasparov's weapon. The little move 5…a6 is deeply flexible: it prepares …e5 or …e6, controls b5, and waits to see White's plan.

Strategy
  • Black: counterattack on the queenside and centre; typical plans include …e5 kicking the knight, …b5–b4, and pressure down the c-file.
  • White: pick a poison — the English Attack (Be3, f3, Qd2, O-O-O, g4), 6.Bg5, or the quiet 6.Be2. Most lines aim to storm the black king.
Hints
  • Know your specific line — the Najdorf punishes vague play with a quick attack.
  • Time your …e5/…e6 and …b5 breaks precisely; a tempo often decides the race.

King's Indian Defense

E60–E99Black
1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 g6 3.Nc3 Bg7 4.e4 d6
Explanation

A fighting hypermodern defence: Black lets White build a huge pawn centre, then attacks it with pieces and a kingside pawn storm. Uncompromising and double-edged — you play for the win with Black.

Strategy
  • Black: castle, play …e5, and if the centre locks, launch …f5–f4–g5–g4 straight at White's king. It's a thematic mating attack.
  • White: expand on the queenside with c5 and b4–b5 and try to break through before Black's kingside storm arrives.
Hints
  • Opposite-wing races: don't get distracted defending — push your attack faster than the opponent pushes theirs.
  • Once the centre is closed, the …f5 break is your green light on the kingside.

Grünfeld Defense

D80Black
1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 g6 3.Nc3 d5
Explanation

The ultimate hypermodern statement: Black invites White to occupy the centre, then chips away at it. After cxd5 Nxd5 and the exchange on c3, White gets a big pawn centre that Black targets with …c5, …Bg7, and heavy piece pressure.

Strategy
  • Black: pressure d4 and the whole centre with …c5, …Nc6/…Bg4, …Qa5 and the g7-bishop raking the long diagonal.
  • White: prove the centre is a strength — support it, develop actively (Be3, Rc1, Qd2), and push for a space-based attack.
Hints
  • Black's whole plan is "attack the centre" — every piece should hit d4 or c3.
  • As White, if the centre falls apart you're worse; keep it defended and mobile.

Nimzo-Indian Defense

E20Black
1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 3.Nc3 Bb4
Explanation

One of the most respected defences to 1.d4. The pin on the c3-knight fights for the centre with pieces and often doubles White's pawns, trading the bishop pair for lasting structural targets.

Strategy
  • Black: often play …Bxc3 to give White doubled c-pawns, then blockade them with …c5, …Nc6/…Na5, …d6 and …e5, working the light squares.
  • White: use the bishop pair and the open lines; play for e4 and a central/kingside initiative to prove dynamism beats structure.
Hints
  • Only trade …Bxc3 when it inflicts real structural damage — otherwise the bishop is precious.
  • The battle is bishop pair & centre (White) versus superior structure & blockade (Black).

Catalan Opening

E00White
1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 3.g3 d5 4.Bg2
Explanation

A Queen's Gambit with the bishop fianchettoed on g2 — combining classical central pressure with a monster long-diagonal bishop. Elite players love its blend of safety and lasting pressure.

Strategy
  • White: apply slow, grinding pressure down the long diagonal and the c-file; often willing to gambit the c4-pawn for a durable positional bind.
  • Black: either hold the c4-pawn with …dxc4 and …b5, or free the game with …c5 and a timely …dxc4 to open lines for the pieces.
Hints
  • The g2-bishop is the soul of the opening — never trade it lightly.
  • The Catalan is a marathon: small, lasting pressure that grinds opponents down.

Semi-Slav Defense

D43Black
1.d4 d5 2.c4 e6 3.Nf3 Nf6 4.Nc3 c6
Explanation

A hyper-solid yet razor-sharp hybrid of the Slav and QGD — Black supports d5 with both …e6 and …c6. It leads to some of the most theory-drenched battlegrounds in chess (the Meran and Botvinnik systems).

Strategy
  • Black: aim for the …dxc4 and …b5 expansion (Meran) to free the bishop and grab queenside space, or the wild pawn-grabbing Botvinnik lines.
  • White: choose between quiet pressure and the sharp 5.Bg5 gambits; central control and development lead the way.
Hints
  • These lines are concrete — study the specific variation you intend to play.
  • The freeing …c5 break (after …b5 and development) is a recurring goal for Black.

King's Gambit

C30White
1.e4 e5 2.f4
Explanation

The great romantic gambit. White sacrifices the f-pawn to rip open the centre and the f-file for a direct attack. Objectively risky, but a fantastic way to learn attacking chess — and dangerous in a real game.

Strategy
  • White: after 2…exf4, develop fast (Nf3, Bc4, O-O, d4) and use the open f-file and lead in development to attack f7 and the king.
  • Black: either hold the extra pawn carefully (…g5 lines) or return it with …d5 for free, healthy development.
Hints
  • As White, development and initiative are everything — never play to just "win the pawn back."
  • As Black, …d5! at the right moment defuses the attack and equalises comfortably.
